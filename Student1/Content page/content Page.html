PROTECTING SEA LIFE FROM PLASTIC WASTE

What’s the Issue with Plastic Waste and Sea Life?

Plastic waste in oceans is a growing threat to marine life. Every year, millions of tons of plastic enter the sea, entangling animals like turtles, seabirds, and whales, or being ingested by fish and other species.

This pollution disrupts ecosystems, introduces toxic chemicals into the food chain, and destroys habitats like coral reefs. Single-use plastics, such as bags and straws, are major contributors to this crisis.

How Can We Protect Sea Life from Plastic Waste?

Protecting sea life starts with reducing plastic use and waste. Switching to reusable bags, bottles, and straws cuts down on single-use plastics that often end up in the ocean.

Participating in beach cleanups removes existing debris, while supporting recycling programs prevents more plastic from reaching waterways. Governments and businesses can enforce stricter regulations on plastic production.

Planting trees and protecting forests absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, while reducing waste and promoting recycling lower methane emissions from landfills. Governments and individuals can also support policies and advocate for sustainable alternatives. Small actions collectively make a big difference in safeguarding marine ecosystems.

Who Is Affected by Plastic Pollution in the Oceans?

Plastic pollution impacts all marine life, from tiny plankton to massive whales, as well as human communities that rely on the ocean. Sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish, leading to fatal blockages,

while fish and birds ingest microplastics, passing toxins up the food chain—eventually reaching humans. Coastal communities face economic losses from dying fisheries and polluted beaches.

The ripple effects threaten biodiversity and global food security, making this an urgent issue for everyone to address.
